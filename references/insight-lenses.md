# Insight lenses

Use these lenses as prompts for analysis. Select the few that fit the news rather than forcing every story through every lens.

## Incentives and interests

- Who gains revenue, control, data, attention, legitimacy, or bargaining power?
- Who pays directly, subsidizes indirectly, or absorbs hidden costs?
- What behavior does the new incentive reward?

## Constraint shift

- Which old bottleneck became cheaper or easier?
- Which new bottleneck now matters more?
- Did the scarce resource move from technology to trust, distribution, judgment, talent, energy, regulation, or time?

## Power and responsibility

- Did decision-making power move to a platform, company, regulator, employer, or user?
- Did responsibility move in the opposite direction from power?
- Who can now set the rules while someone else carries the consequences?

## Second-order effects

- How will competitors, workers, creators, consumers, or regulators adapt?
- What happens after the obvious first benefit is widely adopted?
- Could the solution create a new dependency, market, risk, or inequality?

## Distribution, not averages

- Who experiences the upside first and who waits?
- Does an average improvement hide worse outcomes for a smaller group?
- Are costs concentrated while benefits are spread, or the reverse?

## Price and business model

- If the user is not paying, who funds the service and what do they want in return?
- Is a free or cheap product training behavior before prices, ads, or lock-in increase?
- Does the business model change what the product is optimized to do?

## Behavior and adaptation

- How will people change once they know the new rule or technology exists?
- Will measurement change the behavior being measured?
- Could convenience weaken a skill, habit, relationship, or institution that was previously necessary?

## Trust and legitimacy

- Does the event change what counts as proof, authorship, expertise, authenticity, or consent?
- Who becomes the trusted verifier, and what incentives do they have?
- Can the verification system itself be gamed?

## Timing and path dependence

- Why is this happening now rather than earlier?
- Which earlier decisions made the current outcome likely?
- Is the current event a turning point, a delayed consequence, or only a visible symptom?

## Ordinary-person decision

- What should a worker, consumer, creator, parent, student, or small business owner reconsider?
- Which action is sensible now, and which reaction would be premature?
- What signal would show that the viewpoint is becoming more or less likely?

## Tests for a strong insight

A strong insight should pass most of these tests:

- It remains meaningful after emotional language is removed.
- Its causal chain can be stated clearly.
- It explains more than the single event.
- It makes a limited prediction or decision implication.
- It survives a serious counterargument.
- It is useful even to a reader who already knows the headline.
